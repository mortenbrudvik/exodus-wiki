The Arcadia’s Moon is Andino’s Traveler starship and the novel’s second most important vessel after the Diligent. It carries archon errands, an assassin, a fugitive agitator, and finally a demonstration of force — a single charter hull threading nearly every deniable operation in the book, usually under someone else’s name.

A deniable hull in the Great Game

Traveler ships exist in the gap between Celestial dominions, and the Arcadia’s Moon is the primary on-page example: a working starship whose captain sells passage, salvage muscle, and silence to whoever charters it. Celestial archons use exactly this kind of hull as a deniable sensor — reach without fingerprints. Sahdiah of the Talloch-Te works through it repeatedly, and the ship’s willingness to fly under false identity is part of what it is selling.

Its first significant job is interdiction. Marcellu flies to meet Sahdiah aboard the ship with a brief to keep the Diligent from acquiring a ZPZ generator. At Terrik Papuan the Arcadia’s Moon tails the Lestari carrying Finn and Ellie; fire is exchanged and ghost fleets assault the salvage. The interdiction fails, but the ship comes out of it embedded in the plot.

Carrying Medusa, carrying Josias

After Liliana kills Marcellu with a cherenkov blade, Andino takes Medusa aboard under summons from Sahdiah, who suspects Olomo and sets her to investigate the murder. The ship is where a human-scale killer is handed an inter-archon blame game — the hinge that sends Medusa into Gondiar’s gang networks and eventually into Terence’s custody.

Later it becomes an escape vehicle. When warrants and ghost units move on the Jalgori-Tobus after Josias’s mass strike broadcast, Josias flees Gondiar with Liliana and Toše aboard the Arcadia’s Moon, running as the Infinite Totality. The same hull that was hired to suppress the human rebellion is now carrying its most public voice out of reach.

The Infinite Totality is a false hull rather than a false name. The Arcadia’s Moon does not share the usual Traveler freighter lines: its true shape is a geodesic sphere of golden trusses containing eight ovoid elements that move around inside the sphere and reconfigure their alignment with the ship’s flight status — so concealing it means burying that silhouette. A Lidon astroengineering firm builds the answer to order: a shell fuselage, a cone whose base flares into a sphere, drawn from ultrabonded fibre hunted out of that planet’s ruined geo-ring. The ship flies inside the shell and sheds it once it has served, leaving the casing split in half and hinged open.

The disguise holds well enough to be sold. Terence tries to charter the Infinite Totality as an unrelated vessel to get the Jalgori-Tobus clear, and finds the berth already empty. Even watchers keeping the Arcadia’s Moon on a standing list need a drone to observe the covering before they know the two names are one hull.

Dolod, Capo Frois, and the antimatter demonstration

At Dolod the Arcadia’s Moon observes Gyvoy’s operation on the Archimedes Engine infrastructure, and when the crisis breaks it runs with the arkship rather than against it. The novel’s final beats send both the Diligent and the Arcadia’s Moon vectoring toward Capo Frois — the region Helena-Thyra’s hardline court has been talking about annexing.

Andino’s closing intent gives the ship its last and largest role: a demonstration of a ~120,000 kt antimatter device near Capo Frois. A Traveler charter that began the book taking contracts to suppress a technology ends it threatening a dominion frontier with one.
