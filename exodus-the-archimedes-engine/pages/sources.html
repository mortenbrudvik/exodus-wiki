Sources and provenance

Where this wiki’s content comes from, and which parts of it are reconstructions rather than statements you can rely on. Article pages elsewhere on this wiki state things plainly, without interrupting themselves to discuss their own sourcing; this page is where that information lives instead.

Primary source

The novel itself — Exodus: The Archimedes Engine by Peter F. Hamilton — is the only authority for anything in the story. Every character, place, faction and technology article is an original prose summary written for this wiki. Long quotations from the novel are deliberately avoided.

Where this wiki and the book disagree, the book is right. Corrections are expected.

Reconstructed, not canon

Two parts of this wiki are scaffolding built to make the story navigable. They are useful for finding your place; they are not the novel’s own structure, and they should not be quoted as fact.

Timeline years

The ~42,3xx figures are not dates the novel prints. They are reverse-engineered from in-story “N years ago” dialogue, spoken by characters whose own clocks disagree because of relativistic travel. Treat every year on that page as a working label for ordering events, not as a calendar. The ordering is more trustworthy than the numbers.

Chapter summaries

The numbered rows are sequential summary units invented for this wiki, not the novel’s chapters. The book interleaves many short point-of-view chapters across concurrent threads, and the units group them by thread and beat instead. Numbers will not line up with your copy.

What is not recorded

This wiki was seeded in July 2026, and the material consulted at the time was not logged. The references above were established afterwards and cover the real-world publication facts only — they do not account for the plot, character and setting detail that makes up most of these pages. That detail has not been re-checked against the novel line by line.

So: where an article states a name, a relationship or a sequence of events, it is being reported in good faith but without a citation behind it. The project README records the writing conventions, including the rule that a short accurate stub is preferred over an invented detail. Pages known to be thin say so with an explicit stub notice, as on Boksrock.
