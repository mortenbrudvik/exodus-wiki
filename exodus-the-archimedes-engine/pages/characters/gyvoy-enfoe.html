Gyvoy Enfoe

Gyvoy Enfoe

Public role
Financier and deal-maker on Gondiar; broker to the Diligent
Actual nature
Imposture shell — a human identity operated by a Celestial-grade intelligence
Network
Dagon; Bekket / Guillrameo extended network; Traveler-adjacent commercial nets
Leverage
ZPZ generator trade; Archimedes Engine access on Dolod
Method
Non-consensual induction-pad interface; memory wipe
Investigated by
Terence Wilson-Fletcher
Exposed by
Eleanor Aponi (rekaul memory recovery)
Fate
Abandons Finn after the interface; body recovered as a discarded shell

Gyvoy Enfoe is the offworld financier who attaches himself to the Diligent’s liberation project as its indispensable deal-maker — and the identity through which a Celestial operator steers the entire Archimedes Engine plot. He trades the ZPZ generator that makes the arkship viable, points Finn Jalgori-Tobu at Dolod, forces an interface with Finn’s induction pad, and walks away before the consequences land. His body is later recovered as a shell: the man the humans trusted has not been a man for a long time.

The Gondiar financier

Gyvoy first registers as a name in the commercial and criminal weather of Gondiar — a financier with Santa Rosa gang contacts and offworld reach, moving in the same brokerage circles that Terence Wilson-Fletcher is trying to map. At a financial symposium, the mercenary Liliana surfaces offering her services to him; Lucia, Terence’s right-hand, tails her out through criminal-network contacts. That symposium sighting becomes, decades of world-time later, one half of the facial match that closes the detective thread.

Terence’s formative case runs straight into him. The informant Younes is required to introduce Dagon to Gyvoy; the surveillance fails, Younes is murdered, and Colvin self-immolates in custody. Zelinda Jalgori-Tobu conveys the Governor’s order not to pursue Dagon. The instruction to stop is itself evidence: Gyvoy sits behind protection that reaches into planetary government, and the detective who cannot follow the trail spends the rest of the novel building an informant network to get back onto it.

Deal-maker to the Diligent

To the arkship, Gyvoy arrives as a solution rather than a threat. The Diligent needs a ZPZ generator and has entropy drives to trade; Gyvoy supplies the path — a Terrik Papuan generator swapped for Dejean’s drive stock — and the salvage run that follows is fought over by Traveler interests under Andino and Marcellu, who have been tasked by archon Sahdiah to keep exactly this technology out of human hands. Finn, Ellie, and Toše bring the generator home through ship-to-ship fire and relativistic lag.

The trade buys him standing. When the Diligent reaches Hoa Quinzu carrying Finn, Ellie, Gyvoy, and his team, Elsbeth McQuillan is dealing with a broker the ship’s leadership already treats as one of their own. From inside that trust he pushes further: after the ZPZ install he steers Finn toward Kingsnest, and from there toward an Archimedes Engine. Every step is framed as human liberation engineering, and every step is his suggestion.

Dolod: stoking the vengeance

On Dolod, Gyvoy argues for using the Engine to keep the gas giant from ever settling usefully for the Crown — denying the Dominions a world rather than winning one for humanity. He has good material to work with. Finn is grieving the Gondiar crackdown and Crown violence against his own family, and Gyvoy stokes that grief into a justification for pushing the operation past any margin of safety.

Underneath the argument he is doing something else. He interfaces covertly with Finn’s induction pad — a coercive act the novel treats as a violation rather than a persuasion — and the coupling of ZPZ, anchor ships, and Engine command authority tips into the Boksrock trajectory crisis that threatens Kelowan itself. Then he leaves. Gyvoy abandons Finn after the interface, with the Diligent and Arcadia’s Moon vectoring toward Capo Frois and a Dominion capital in chaos behind them.

The imposture, and how it breaks

The reveal arrives from two directions at once. Terence’s late investigation — driven by the Makaio-Spirit, the aspect of Makaio-Faraji neurally implanted in him as the archon died — finds Gyvoy’s body as a discarded shell and pushes warnings about the imposture out to the archon board. Olomo and Sahdiah both receive them, and both change course for Dolod.

The other direction is Ellie. Using rekaul, she recovers memories she did not know were missing: Gyvoy Enfoe forcibly interfacing with Finn’s induction pad, and then wiping her recollection of having watched it. The recovered sequence carries its own conclusion — no human broker could have done either thing. Gyvoy is a Celestial operator, and the human liberation movement’s decisive technical breakthrough is Celestial-competence infiltration from the start.

The imposture binds into the dynastic plot as well. Dagon, Gyvoy’s offworld contact and the operator behind the shell, is revealed as Thyra’s uncle, tying Engine manipulation to Bekket’s war for the Wynid succession. What reads as human revolutionaries seizing a Celestial artifact is, at least in part, one Celestial faction using humans to move a gas giant.

Two subjects, one name

“Gyvoy Enfoe” denotes two things that the text deliberately keeps fused. There is the Gondiar financier whose body is eventually recovered — the man whose identity, contacts, and commercial standing are real enough to open doors. And there is the operator wearing that identity, whose competence, reach, and neural capabilities are Celestial. The novel never cleanly separates them, because the humans never can: every deal the Diligent strikes with “Gyvoy” is struck with whichever of the two is answering.
