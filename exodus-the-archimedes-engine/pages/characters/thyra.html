Thyra is a congregant daughter of Queen Helena-Chione who rises through the Wynid trials of combat, survival, and politics, seizes the mindline, and rules as Helena-Thyra. Hardline, cunning, and willing to murder rivals through neural and bloodline tricks, she drives aggressive Dominion policy and is entangled — via family (Bekket, Dagon) and fleet power — in the Dolod / Boksrock endgame that closes the novel.

Trials and ascent

Born after Helena’s liaison with Lord Bekket around a Dominion coronation, Thyra shows early ruthlessness among congregant sisters (including physical dominance over Clavissa). Across formal trials she eliminates competitors: neural manipulation of mounts in a combat trial; use of non-awakened bloodlines to kill during a survival trial on Binji while saving Clavissa for political cover; and a politics trial at Queen Ramona-Ursule’s court where gossip and financial forensics win her elevation to princess. Helena then gifts her deep mindline knowledge.

Thyra inherits a hereditary trait allowing her to shield thoughts when interfacing with the queen — crucial to surviving scrutiny. Bekket later reveals aspects of her true heritage that reframe her ambitions. Uulana-Lyon, the court parent whose daughter Delfina dies on Binji, investigates Bekket and Thyra; Bekket’s Guillrameo network answers with a slowball ambush and his assassination.

Mindline hijack and hardline rule

Thyra ambushes Helena in a steam room and hijacks her mindline, becoming Helena-Thyra and justifying the coup as necessary leadership against Mara Yama activity. She reshapes the court: Bekket as master of court; hardline archons (including the infiltrated figure of Uulana-Shoigu); Clavissa relegated to nanny duties for younger congregants; residual Helena princesses compelled into naval service. Former chief archon Gahiji is displaced and later works quietly with residual loyalists.

Policy turns expansionist and cynical: proposals to annex Capo Frois and the Uthara Dominion, false-flag destabilization, and massing queen fleets at Kelowan years ahead of a supposed Mara Yama confrontation. Human unrest on Gondiar is used as pretext for navy lockdowns that feed the very rebellion they claim to suppress.

Dolod, Boksrock, and the endgame

Thyra brings her fleet early to Kelowan under cover of eagerness to join imperial exercises. When Finn and allies use an Archimedes Engine on Dolod, anchor ships cage Boksrock instead of delivering a clean Kelowan-friendly outcome. Thyra is initially surprised that Boksrock is aimed at Kelowan. Meanwhile other plots — the kestrel sprites above the imperial palace and the Gyvoy/Dagon networks tied to her wider family — devastate Empress Carolien-Amaia’s position.

How much of the Engine trajectory Thyra plans in advance, and how much she simply exploits, is never resolved. She wants Finn captured for narrative control of the “human rebel” story, issues kill orders when the Diligent flees, and celebrates Carolien’s fall — but Boksrock’s final geometry may exceed her script. The novel ends with Helena-Thyra strengthened amid Dominion chaos and the Uranic resistance still in motion.
