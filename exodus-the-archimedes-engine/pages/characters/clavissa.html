Clavissa

Clavissa is a congregant daughter of Queen Helena-Chione and consort Lord Jolav-Dabny — a survivor of Wynid’s lethal succession culture. Sibling to the rising Thyra, she is slapped aside in childhood flight training, spared on Binji when Thyra needs a “saved sister,” demoted to nanny after the mindline coup, and quietly recruited by Jolav to spy on Helena-Thyra’s early Kelowan fleet with help from Neusch (Makaio line).

Court dynamics among congregants

Congregant daughters of a Now and Forever Queen compete through staged trials of combat, survival, and politics for elevation toward princess status and mindline gifts. Clavissa is present from childhood scenes of rivalry — including a flight-training session where Thyra slaps her aside — and through later trials where Thyra’s tactics leave other sisters dead. In the survival trial on Binji, Thyra arranges killings via non-awakened bloodlines yet spares Clavissa, banking political credit for “saving” a sister while removing stronger threats.

Clavissa also appears at the politics trial among multi-house congregants at Queen Ramona-Ursule’s court. She does not win elevation the way Thyra does; her narrative function is the court insider who remains after the ambitious sibling takes everything.

Survival under Thyra

After Thyra hijacks Helena’s mindline and rules as Helena-Thyra, Clavissa is positioned as nanny to younger congregants and princesses — a soft confinement that keeps her visible, useful, and non-threatening. She confides distrust of Thyra to her father Jolav-Dabny. When Thyra’s fleet leaves early for Kelowan, Jolav asks Clavissa to spy and points her to Neusch (linked to the Makaio archon line) for help once in-system.

During the naval endgame she shares information with Neusch while he continues investigations toward Uixic. Clavissa’s arc is survival politics: she neither seizes the crown nor flees the court, but she keeps a channel of resistance open inside a regime that rewards hardline loyalty and punishes open dissent. Compared with Thyra’s spectacular violence, Clavissa’s importance is continuity — a living alternative memory of Helena’s court that the usurpation never fully erases.
